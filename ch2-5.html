Chapter 2.5 · Appendix

Which AI Tool for Which Job?

There are three kinds of AI tool you'll hear about. They overlap, and the names change every few months, so learn the kinds, not the brand names.

By the end of this appendix you can

  • Tell a chat assistant, a coding agent and a bot platform apart.
  • Pick the simplest kind of tool that can do a job.
  • Use a second AI as a critic, while you stay the one who decides.

The three kinds

KindWhat it's forWhat it does
1 · Chat assistant
e.g. ChatGPT, Grok, Google Gemini, Claude		Thinking, planning, explainingYou type, it answers. It doesn't change your project's files by itself; you copy things in and out.
2 · Coding agent
e.g. Cursor, Google Antigravity		Changes to real files in your projectOpens your project folder, reads the files, makes changes, and can run checks.
3 · Bot platform
e.g. Grok Bot (case study only)		Ongoing jobs and connected appsDoes a job on a schedule, connects to apps like email or a task list, and hands work between bots.

Chat assistants. Use one to brainstorm, write a spec, explain an error message, or review a plan. Several have offered free versions, but plans change often, so check before class.

Coding agents. Some have offered limited free plans; check before class. Because a coding agent can change real files, it needs clear rules (see 2.7) and undo history (Chapter 3).

Bot platforms. This is what runs the bot team in the ALC build. You don't need one for this course; it appears here as a case study only. Chapter 5 covers automation with free tools.

Age rules and school approval

Some AI tools are for adults only, and some plans have age limits. Check your school's rules and each tool's age rules, and use only tools your school has approved (see Chapter 4).

Rule of thumb

Start with the simplest tool that can do the job. Move up only when you need to. AI researcher Andrej Karpathy describes an "autonomy slider": you choose how much independence to give an AI tool, depending on how hard the task is, and keep a person checking the work (Y Combinator, 2025).

Should you pass a plan between several AIs?

I asked this myself while writing this guide. The honest answer: yes, once or twice, with a human in charge.

Why it helps. A second AI hasn't seen the first one's reasoning, so it isn't attached to its choices. Researchers found that when several AI chats critique each other's answers over a few rounds, the answers tend to get more accurate (Du et al., 2024). Asking one AI to simply recheck its own work is weaker: without outside feedback, it often doesn't improve (Huang et al., 2024). It's the same idea as the builder and reviewer chats in 2.4.

How to do it well

  1. One AI drafts. Use the six-line template from 2.6.
  2. A different AI critiques. Paste the draft and ask: "What's unclear, missing, or likely to go wrong? Rank your top five."
  3. You decide. Accept, change or reject each point. The AIs give advice; you're the one who knows the real need.
  4. Keep one source of truth. One plan document (a Google Doc works). Every accepted change goes into that doc, not into five versions in five chats.
  1. AI 1 drafts
  2. AI 2 critiques
  3. You decideaccept or reject each point
  4. One plan doc
Avoid endless ping-pong

Each AI will always find something to change, and the plan can drift away from what you actually wanted. Stop after one or two rounds, or as soon as the feedback is just small wording changes.

This guide did exactly that

The plan for this course went through two rounds of outside feedback. Each round was merged into one plan document, I decided which edits to accept, and the chapters were written from that one doc.

Try it · 15 minutes

  1. Take your Chapter 1 spec. Ask one chat assistant to improve it.
  2. Paste the result into a different assistant (or a fresh chat) and ask for the top five problems.
  3. Pick which points you accept, and update your one plan doc.

Timing

30-45 minutes, or set as homework. It fits in the optional third lesson of Chapter 2.

Common sticking points

  • Students treat the second AI as the judge. Remind them the human decides.
  • Some schools allow only one assistant. A fresh chat in the same assistant, given only the draft, is an acceptable stand-in.

How to assess

  • Every accepted or rejected point has a reason.
  • There is one up-to-date plan doc.
